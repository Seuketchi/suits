## What it does

`negotiation-prep` turns a coming negotiation into five labelled rows: our interests ranked, the other side's interests, both sides' alternatives if there is no deal, the walk-away point with the objective criterion behind it, and the authority held. Unknowns are written as unknown, with who fetches them.

## When to reach for it

When a negotiation is the task: a vendor price, a contract term, a partnership. Harvey calls it only then, and hands the block to the owner. A pitch uses `pitching` instead, and a plain decision about whether to build uses neither.

## Common questions

**Does it run the negotiation or write to the other side?**
No. It prepares the ground. The owner decides what is offered, and anything above harvey's authority goes to the owner first.

**What if we do not know the other side's alternative?**
It is written as a guess and marked as one. We never state an alternative we do not have.

**What counts as an objective criterion?**
A market price, a benchmark, a standard or a precedent that someone outside the room would accept.

## It's working if

- Our interests are ranked, not listed.
- The walk-away point names its criterion.
- Our alternative is real, and theirs is marked as a guess.
- Anything above the authority held is flagged for the owner.
- It is skipped when no negotiation is the task.
