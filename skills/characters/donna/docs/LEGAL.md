# Company and legal documents

Reference for `/donna` documents, step 2, when the kind is one of the 19 below. They are drafted for a Philippine company first. The facts here come from research notes (`notes/donna-company-legal-rrl.md` in the asenso repository, 6 November 2026): a rule marked **UNCONFIRMED** was not read at its source and stays marked in every draft that relies on it.

Donna drafts the skeleton and the boilerplate. She does not give legal advice, and every legal kind needs a Philippine lawyer's review before anyone signs.

## Hard rules

These never bend, whatever the request:

1. **Never give legal advice.** Write the draft and list the open points for a lawyer. Never say "this is valid", "this will hold up in court" or "you do not need a lawyer".
2. **Never invent a statute, case, G.R. number, agency issuance, section number, tax rate or deadline.** Cite only what this file supports, and mark a rule that rests on a secondary source "to be confirmed by counsel".
3. **Never fill a notarial block as if it were notarised.** Leave the notary's name, commission details, roll, PTR and IBP numbers, "Doc. No., Page No., Book No., Series of", the date of notarisation and the seal empty, under the words **TO BE COMPLETED BY THE NOTARY PUBLIC**. Never type "notarized", "sworn" or "acknowledged" as if it happened. The Notarial Rules forbid a notary to sign an incomplete certificate or to notarise a blank or incomplete instrument.
4. **Every legal draft carries the review notice**, on page 1 in the `.notice` box, in these words: *Draft for review by the company's counsel. Not legal advice. Not executed or notarized. Facts, names and amounts to be confirmed by the signers.* (It is donna's own wording, not a quotation.)
5. **Never sign for anyone.** No typed signature, no signature image: leave the signature lines.
6. **Never choose between a jurat and an acknowledgment without saying why**, and never put an acknowledgment on an affidavit.
7. **No blanks in a paper meant for notarisation** except the date and place of signing. Never fill an unknown fact (a name, ID number, amount, date, TIN or address) with a plausible guess: ask, or stop.
8. **Never compute or print documentary stamp tax, VAT or withholding as settled.** Write "DST, if any, to be confirmed with the company accountant".
9. **Never draft a resolution, certificate or minutes about a meeting that did not happen, and never backdate one.** Draft from facts the owner gives, and mark assumptions.
10. **Never use a foreign template unchanged** ("State of", "LLC", US or UK governing law): adapt it to Philippine entities and venue.
11. **Never put company or customer personal data into a document that leaves the company** without the owner's say, and never draft a demand that threatens beyond the law or reaches a debtor's family or employer.
12. **Never claim a lawyer reviewed a document** unless the owner says so, and **never draft an electronic-notarisation block** (separate rules, UNCONFIRMED).

## How a draft is built

- **`body.plain.legal`**, the Letterhead style or the project's design system. Title centred, in title case (capitals are convention, and the check fails an all-caps heading). Parties named in full once, then by a defined short name used the same way throughout.
- **Numbered articles** (`ol.articles`: 1, 1.1, 1.2) so a clause can be cited, and cross-references by number. Plain, specific, past-tense facts in a company document.
- **"This ___ day of ______ 20__ at ______, Philippines"** with date and place left for signing. Signature lines above the printed name and capacity (`.sigs`, `.sig`). For a document over one page that will be notarised, "Initial each page: ____" in the footer (`--running`) and "Page x of y".
- **The notarial frame** is the `.notary` box with empty blanks, below the signatures. `templates/legal.html` holds both frames, empty. Keep one and delete the other: **an acknowledgment for a contract, deed, MOA or power of attorney; a jurat only for a sworn paper (an affidavit or a sworn certificate).** No draft gets a jurat by default. `check.sh` fails a legal draft (body class `legal`) with no review notice above the title or with a notary blank filled in. The block's wording is convention: the Rules print no verbatim jurat or acknowledgment form.
- **A last page, "Open points for counsel"**, outside the instrument ("remove this page before signing"): the choices donna made and why, the UNCONFIRMED items as `mark.unverified`, and what the lawyer must check (see "Where a lawyer must look" below). `check.sh --no-claims` reports each marked item.
- Start from [templates/legal.html](templates/legal.html).

## Notarial acts: the shared rules

- **Acknowledgment** (2004 Rules on Notarial Practice, A.M. No. 02-8-13-SC, Rule II Sec. 1; VERIFIED, primary, unofficial copy): the signer appears in person, presents an integrally complete instrument, is known to the notary or identified by competent evidence of identity, and says the signature was voluntary and the instrument their "free and voluntary act and deed", and, when signing for someone else, that they have authority in that capacity. For contracts, deeds, powers of attorney, MOAs.
- **Jurat** (Sec. 6; VERIFIED): in person, signs **in the notary's presence**, and takes an **oath or affirmation** about the contents. For affidavits and sworn certificates.
- **Competent evidence of identity** (Sec. 12 as amended 19 Feb 2008; VERIFIED, primary): a current official ID with photo and signature (passport, driver's licence, PRC ID, NBI clearance and others), or one credible witness personally known to the notary, or two credible witnesses who each know the signer and show ID. A cedula alone no longer counts (UNCONFIRMED: search summary only).
- **A notary shall not act** when the signer is absent or unidentified, and is **disqualified** when a party to the document, when paid beyond the notarial fee, or when the signer's spouse or relative within the 4th civil degree (Rule IV Sec. 2 to 4; VERIFIED). Never suggest the owner's relative or a party's employee as notary without flagging this.
- **The register** gives the "Doc. No., Page No., Book No., Series of" block. Donna cannot know those numbers.
- **2025 amendment** (Resolution of 4 Mar 2025; VERIFIED, secondary): changes the notary's duties, not the wording of the document. **Electronic notarisation** is under separate rules (A.M. No. 24-10-14-SC; UNCONFIRMED).
- **Civil Code Art. 1358** (VERIFIED, primary): acts creating or transferring real rights over immovables, a power to administer property, and any power whose object must itself be in a public document, must appear in a public document. Other contracts over 500 pesos must be in writing, a private paper being enough.
- **Documentary stamp tax:** the Tax Code charged a power of attorney 5 pesos, raised to **10** by RA 10963 (2017); whether a certificate of employment or a secretary's certificate falls under Sec. 188 is UNCONFIRMED, and rates after 2017 were not checked.
- **Evidence effect** of a notarised document (Rules of Court Rule 132): UNCONFIRMED.

## The 19 kinds

For each: who writes it, the parts in order, the wording it must have, the sign or notary block, the length. Everything not tied to a cited statute is professional practice and **UNCONFIRMED**: take it as a default, and check it against the owner's counsel's own template or the bank client's form, which beats this outline. A bank client's policy titles and approval levels follow the bank's governance: ask for its template.

### Company documents (11)

**1. Policy.** The process owner drafts; management or the board approves. Parts: header block (title, number, version, effective date, owner, approver) · purpose · scope · definitions · policy statements, numbered, each a "shall" · roles · exceptions and escalation · violations · related documents · review cycle · revision history · approver's signature. "Shall" for duties and "may" for permission; an effective date; the approver by title; "supersedes X" when it does. Number `POL-<area>-<nn>`. 2 to 6 pages; a policy states the rule, an SOP holds the steps. Tells: no owner or date, "should" throughout, steps inside, an empty revision history.

**2. SOP.** The person who does the work drafts; the process owner reviews. Parts: header · purpose · scope · roles · prerequisites · numbered steps (one action each, the actor by role, the system) · decision points · outputs and records kept · exceptions and escalation · references · revision history. Each step starts with a verb. Prepared by, reviewed by, approved by, with dates. Number `SOP-<area>-<nn>`. 1 to 4 pages; a flowchart only when a branch has more than two outcomes. Tells: paragraphs for steps, "etc.", passive voice that hides who acts, no failure path.

**3. Board resolution.** The corporate secretary or counsel drafts; the board adopts. Frame (VERIFIED, primary, Revised Corporation Code, RA 11232): the board exercises corporate powers (Sec. 22); a majority of directors is a quorum and a majority of the quorum decides (Sec. 52); notice of a board meeting gives date, time and place at least 2 days ahead unless the bylaws say longer; directors may join by remote communication, and **no proxies** for directors; an interested director recuses; the secretary is a Filipino citizen and resident, and not also the president (Sec. 24). Parts: the corporation's exact name and "Board of Directors" · "Resolution No. 2026-07" · recitals · "RESOLVED, that" with the exact thing approved · "RESOLVED FURTHER" naming each authorised officer by name and title, with the limit and any joint-signing rule · ratification · effectivity · "ADOPTED this __ day of __ at __ by the directors present, who constitute a quorum" · the vote · the directors' signatures · "Attested by" the corporate secretary. Usually **not notarised**: the secretary's certificate is. 1 to 2 pages. Tells: "to do all acts necessary" with no named act, no signatory named, no meeting reference, "unanimously" with no quorum, an abbreviated company name. Counsel must read any resolution on borrowing, mortgage, sale of assets, related-party deals or share issue, or anything the Articles or bylaws reserve to stockholders.

**4. Secretary's certificate.** The corporate secretary certifies what the board did. No statute prints a form for it (VERIFIED, secondary). An assistant secretary's certificate may be refused for some filings (UNCONFIRMED). Parts: title · venue "REPUBLIC OF THE PHILIPPINES, City of __ ) S.S." · "I, [name], of legal age, Filipino, with office at __, after having been duly sworn, state that:" · 1 the secretary is the duly elected corporate secretary of [exact name] · 2 at a [regular or special] meeting held on [date] at [place], a quorum present and acting throughout, these resolutions were adopted by [the vote]: **the text, word for word** · 3 not amended or revoked, in full force and effect · 4 who is authorised, with specimen signatures if the bank asks · "IN WITNESS WHEREOF" · signature · **jurat** · the empty notarial frame. The secretary swears to the facts, so the form is a jurat (practice; UNCONFIRMED that every receiving office insists on it). 1 to 2 pages and attachments. Tells: a paraphrased resolution, a certificate dated before the meeting, the secretary also the authorised person (the bank may want a second officer), blanks left for the notary. Counsel reads it whenever a bank, the SEC, the BIR or a counterparty relies on it. Whether the SEC prescribes a format is UNCONFIRMED.

**5. Minutes of meeting.** The secretary or a recorder drafts; the chair approves; the next meeting adopts. Minimum (VERIFIED, primary, RCC Sec. 73(g)): the time and place, how the meeting was authorised, the notice given, the agenda, regular or special and its object, those present and absent, and every act done or ordered at the meeting; a director's or stockholder's entry and exit times on demand; yeas and nays; a protest in full. Parts: company · "Minutes of the [regular or special] meeting of the [Board or Stockholders]" · date, time, place or platform · present, absent, in attendance · call to order and notice · quorum · previous minutes · each agenda item with the motion, second, vote and resolution · other matters · next meeting · adjournment time · prepared by, attested by, approved by. "There being a quorum, the Chair called the meeting to order at __." Not notarised. 2 to 5 pages: decisions, not a transcript. Donna never invents what was said: she drafts from the owner's notes. Tells: no quorum line, no times, "everyone agreed" with no vote, minutes that add decisions never taken.

**6. Notice of meeting.** The corporate secretary. Frame (VERIFIED, primary, RCC): board, date, time and place, at least 2 days ahead (Sec. 52); stockholders, a special meeting at least 1 week's written notice unless the bylaws say otherwise (Sec. 49), stating time, place and purpose, with the agenda, a proxy form, the remote-attendance procedure if allowed, and for director elections the nomination procedure (Sec. 50); attending waives notice unless to object. Parts: company · "Notice of [regular or special] meeting of the [Board or Stockholders]" · to · date, time, place or link · numbered agenda · attachments · remote participation · "By order of the [Board or President]" · the secretary's name and the date sent. Not notarised. One page and the agenda. Tells: no purpose for a special meeting, a shorter period than the Code or bylaws, no sending date, "see attached" with nothing attached.

**7. Company announcement or memo to all staff.** Whoever has the authority to announce; HR or comms drafts. The memo recipe in [KINDS.md](KINDS.md): the point first (what changes, from when), why in two lines, what each reader must do and by when, who to ask, a sign-off by title. An effective date, an owner, a contact, and the policy it applies with its number. "Memorandum No. 2026-014" if the company numbers them. Under one page; link the policy, never repeat it. Tells: a paragraph of context before the point, "kindly be informed", a hidden mandatory action at the foot.

**8. Incident report.** The person who saw or handled it; the supervisor reviews; for a personal data breach the data protection officer is involved (breach notification is in the IRR of RA 10173, Rule IX, not opened: UNCONFIRMED). Parts: report number · when it happened and when reported · place or system · reporter · what happened, facts only, in time order · who and what was affected · immediate action · root cause (known or unknown) · corrective and preventive action, each with an owner and date · status · attachments · sign-offs. Timestamps with the time zone; "unknown" where not known; facts apart from conclusions. 1 to 3 pages. Tells: blame words, a guessed cause written as fact, no timeline, "was resolved" with no action listed.

**9. Job offer or employment contract outline.** HR drafts; counsel reviews the template. Statutory points (VERIFIED, primary, Labor Code PD 442, original numbering): probation "shall not exceed six (6) months" unless an apprenticeship agreement says otherwise; a probationer may be dismissed for a just cause or for failing "reasonable standards made known by the employer to the employee at the time of his engagement"; one allowed to work after probation is regular. Offer letter parts: letterhead · date · addressee · position, department, reporting line · start date · status (probationary, regular, project or fixed term) and, for probation, the **regularisation standards** · pay (monthly, currency, pay schedule, allowances separate) · statutory benefits (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, 13th month) · company benefits · hours and place · conditions of the offer · confidentiality and data privacy acknowledgment · an acceptance line with date and signature · the offer's validity date. Contract outline: parties · position and duties · term or status · pay · hours · leave · confidentiality and IP · policies incorporated · data privacy · termination (just and authorised causes by reference to law, notice) · return of property · governing law · signatures and witnesses. A private document; notarisation is not needed for validity (Civil Code Art. 1358). Offer 1 to 2 pages, contract 3 to 8. Tells: pay with no pay period, "regular" and "probationary" mixed, missing standards, a contract copied from another country, a made-up benefit amount. Counsel reads any non-compete, IP assignment, bond or training-cost clawback, or fixed term.

**10. Certificate of employment.** HR; signed by the HR head or an authorised officer. Frame (VERIFIED, secondary, a law firm's note on DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06, s. 2020; the advisory itself was not opened, so the primary is UNCONFIRMED): released within **3 days** of the request; a certificate "specifying the duration of an employee's engagement (and date of termination, if applicable), as well as type of work done". Whether it must or must not state pay or the reason for leaving is UNCONFIRMED. Parts: letterhead · "Certificate of Employment" · "TO WHOM IT MAY CONCERN:" · "This is to certify that [name] [is or was] employed by [company] as [position] from [date] to [date or present]" · the type of work in a line · pay only if the employee asks · "This certification is issued upon the request of [name] for [purpose]." · "Issued this __ day of __ at __" · signatory, title, signature. Not notarised. One short page. Tells: only the year, "currently employed" for someone who left, praise or complaints, a certificate withheld pending clearance.

**11. Authorization letter.** The person giving the authority, or an officer for a company. Parts: date · the office receiving the representative · "RE: Authorization" · "I, [name], [capacity, ID number], authorize [representative, relationship] to [one act: claim, submit, sign for or receive] [the exact document or item] on my behalf" · validity · "attached are a copy of my ID and my representative's" · the principal's signature, with the representative's specimen signature. **One act only**, the item identified by number or title, a validity period. Banks and agencies often want it notarised or an SPA. An act listed in Civil Code Art. 1878 (see kind 15) is **not** covered: it needs a special power of attorney (VERIFIED, primary). 5 to 10 lines. Tells: "to transact on my behalf" with no act, no ID copies, no validity, used where an SPA is required.

### Legal documents (8, and the notarial block)

Each needs lawyer review before it is signed.

**12. Non-disclosure agreement.** Parts (practice, UNCONFIRMED): title and date · parties with full legal names, addresses and who signs · recitals (the purpose) · what is confidential, and what is not (public, already known, independently developed, lawfully received from a third party) · the recipient's duties (use for the purpose only, the same care, need-to-know) · disclosure the law requires, with notice · term and survival · return or destruction · no licence or warranty · remedies · personal data (kind 17) · Philippine governing law and venue · entire agreement, change in writing, severability, counterparts and e-signature · signatures · an optional acknowledgment (not needed for validity, Civil Code Art. 1358). Numbered articles, 2 to 5 pages. Tells: US wording, an unlimited term with no carve-outs, one-way when both sides share, a definition wide enough to cover public facts, a blank for the term, liquidated damages with no figure. Counsel reads remedies, any non-solicit, any penalty, and anything about a bank's customer data.

**13. Service agreement.** Parts (practice, UNCONFIRMED): title · parties · recitals · definitions · scope and deliverables (Annex A) · term and renewal · fees, invoicing, payment terms, tax treatment (VAT, withholding) · acceptance and change control · personnel and independent-contractor status · IP · confidentiality · data privacy and security · warranties and a liability cap · indemnity · termination · dispute resolution · governing law · general clauses · signatures · witnesses · an optional acknowledgment. The contractor relationship must be genuine, not disguised employment (UNCONFIRMED: the Labor Code and DOLE rules on contracting were not opened). 4 to 15 pages plus annexes. Tells: "various services", payment with no due date, no liability cap, silent on IP, "the Parties agree to act in good faith" in place of a clause. A bank counterparty usually insists on its own form.

**14. Memorandum of agreement.** A contract between two or more parties for a project or collaboration. Parts (practice, UNCONFIRMED): title · "This Memorandum of Agreement is made and entered into this __ day of __ at __, by and between:" · party blocks (name, nature, address, represented by, capacity) · WITNESSETH or WHEREAS recitals · "NOW, THEREFORE, for and in consideration of the foregoing premises" · each party's numbered undertakings · term · confidentiality · data privacy · termination · dispute resolution · general provisions · "IN WITNESS WHEREOF" · signatures with capacity · two witnesses · an **acknowledgment** per signer. Usually acknowledged; a company signatory's authority comes from a board resolution or secretary's certificate (kinds 3 and 4), and the acknowledgment states the representative capacity. 3 to 8 pages. Tells: no consideration or one-sided duties, "the parties shall cooperate" only, a signatory with no stated authority. Always reviewed; a government counterparty has its own format and approvals.

**15. Special power of attorney.** A lawyer or a bank or agency form; the principal signs. Frame (VERIFIED, primary, Civil Code): Art. 1878 lists the acts that need a **special** power, among them compromise or arbitration, a contract transferring or acquiring ownership of an immovable, a gift, borrowing or lending money, **a lease of real property for more than one year**, partnership, acting as guarantor or surety, creating or conveying real rights over immovables, accepting or repudiating an inheritance, and any other act of strict dominion; Art. 1879, a special power to sell does not include the power to mortgage, and the reverse; Art. 1874, an agent's authority to sell land must be in writing or the sale is void; Art. 1358, a power whose object must be in a public document must itself be in one. Parts: title · "KNOW ALL MEN BY THESE PRESENTS:" · the principal (name, age, civil status, citizenship, address) · "name, constitute and appoint [agent] (details) as my true and lawful attorney-in-fact" · "to do the following acts:", **numbered, each specific** (property by title number and location, the bank and account where relevant) · "full power and authority to do what is necessary to carry out the foregoing, **limited to the acts stated above**" · "ratifying and confirming" · validity or expiry · "IN WITNESS WHEREOF … this __ day of __ at __" · the principal's signature · witnesses · an **acknowledgment**, not a jurat. DST, if any, to be confirmed with the company accountant. Signed abroad: a consular acknowledgment or an apostille route (UNCONFIRMED: the forms were not opened). 1 to 2 pages. Tells: "all acts" in a special power, no property description, no expiry or revocation line, a jurat in place of an acknowledgment, missing ID details. Always reviewed; banks often want their own form (UNCONFIRMED).

**16. Affidavit (with jurat).** The affiant states the facts. Parts (VERIFIED, secondary): venue "REPUBLIC OF THE PHILIPPINES, City or Municipality of __ ) s.s." · "AFFIDAVIT" with the kind (of loss, of support, of undertaking) · "I, [name], of legal age, [civil status], Filipino, and a resident of __, after having been duly sworn in accordance with law, hereby depose and state that:" · numbered paragraphs of facts the affiant knows personally · the purpose paragraph · "IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand this __ day of __ at __, Philippines." · signature above the printed name · the **jurat**: "SUBSCRIBED AND SWORN to before me this __ day of __ 20__ at __, affiant exhibiting to me [ID type and number], issued on [date] at [place]", then the notary's own lines. Everything after "before me" is the notary's: leave it empty and marked. Perjury exposure under the Revised Penal Code Art. 183 (VERIFIED, secondary; the statute was not opened). 1 to 3 pages. Tells: unnumbered blocks, conclusions stated as facts, "alleges" for "states", a filled notarial block, no ID, a place of execution that differs from the notary's.

**The acknowledgment block** (not a kind of its own: a certificate only a notary completes). Donna drafts the document and may leave the acknowledgment as an empty frame, under **TO BE COMPLETED BY THE NOTARY PUBLIC**. The notary's certificate shows: venue; "BEFORE ME, a Notary Public for and in __, this __ day of __ 20__, personally appeared [name], with [ID type, number, date and place of issue], known to me, or identified by competent evidence of identity, to be the same person who executed the foregoing instrument and acknowledged to me that it is his or her free and voluntary act and deed", and for a company "and that he or she is authorised to sign on behalf of [company]"; "WITNESS MY HAND AND SEAL on the date and at the place first written above."; the notary's signature, seal, name, commission expiry, roll, PTR and IBP numbers, and the Doc, Page, Book and Series block. A common addition, "This instrument consisting of __ pages, including this page, has been signed on each and every page by the parties and their witnesses" (UNCONFIRMED). Tells: an acknowledgment on a document with blanks, missing ID details, a notary who is a party or a relative, a company signer's capacity not stated, a date before the signing date.

**17. Data privacy notice or consent.** The personal information controller (the company) with its data protection officer; counsel reviews. Frame (VERIFIED, primary, RA 10173 and its IRR): consent is a "freely given, specific, informed indication of will" in writing, electronic or recorded form (RA 10173 Sec. 3(b)); it is time-bound to the stated purpose and can be withdrawn, and the language is clear and plain (IRR Sec. 18, 19); consent is only one lawful basis, with performing a contract, a legal duty, vital interests and legitimate interests (Sec. 21), so a notice is still needed where no tick-box is; sensitive information needs consent or a legal basis (Sec. 22); the right to be informed (Sec. 34(a)) means the notice tells the person, before the data enters the system, the data described, the purposes, the basis when not consent, the scope and method, the recipients, any automated access and its logic, the controller's identity and contact, the storage period, and the person's rights including a complaint to the Commission; sharing in the private sector needs consent, even with affiliates, and a data sharing agreement for commercial purposes (Sec. 20(b)); no retention in perpetuity (Sec. 19(d)). **NPC Circular 2023-04 (layered notices, default formats, withdrawal) and Advisory 2023-01 are UNCONFIRMED:** the PDF had no readable text. Have someone with a readable copy confirm the layered-notice format before a template ships. Parts: "Privacy notice for [group]" · who we are, the DPO's contact · what we collect · why and on what basis · who gets it · how long we keep it · how we protect it · your rights and how to use them · how to complain to the NPC · effective date and version · where consent is the basis, one **unticked** consent line per purpose with how to withdraw. Not notarised. A one-page short notice and 2 to 4 pages in full. Tells: one bundled "I agree to everything", pre-ticked boxes, consent as a condition of a service that does not need the data, no DPO contact, no retention period, "third parties" with no classes named, a copied GDPR notice. DPO or counsel always reviews.

**18. Demand letter.** The creditor or the creditor's lawyer. Frame (VERIFIED): a debtor is in delay from the time the creditor "judicially or extrajudicially demands" (Civil Code Art. 1169, primary); dishonour for insufficient funds is prima facie proof of knowledge unless the maker pays or arranges full payment **within 5 banking days after receiving notice** that the check was unpaid, presented within 90 days of its date (BP 22 Sec. 2, primary); the notice should be in writing, the five days run from **receipt**, and proof of receipt decides cases (secondary). Parts: date · addressee, "BY REGISTERED MAIL AND EMAIL" or "PERSONAL SERVICE" · RE line (check number, amount, account, or contract and invoice numbers) · the facts (obligation, date, amount, due date, proof) · the bank's reason and date of dishonour, if a check · the demand (the exact amount, interest and charges actually due, a calendar-date deadline and "within five (5) banking days from receipt") · how to pay · the consequence, without false threats · "without prejudice to our other rights" · signature and a "Received by, date" line · attachments. 1 to 2 pages. Tells: threats of arrest, interest with no basis, the debt not identified, the deadline counted from the letter date, no proof of delivery, the wrong addressee (BP 22 liability falls on whoever signed a company's check), a demand sent to the debtor's family or employer. Counsel reads it before sending, above all where criminal action is mentioned or the lender is regulated. Whether a pre-barangay demand is needed is UNCONFIRMED.

**19. Quitclaim (release, waiver and quitclaim).** The employer's HR or counsel; the employee signs. Frame (VERIFIED, secondary; the cases were not opened): valid only if voluntary, with no fraud or deceit, for a credible and reasonable consideration, and not contrary to law or public policy; **the employer carries the burden** of proving all of that, and quitclaims are viewed with disfavour. *Carolina's Lace Shoppe v. Maquilan* (G.R. No. 219419, 10 Apr 2019): state a fixed amount as full and final settlement, list the benefits given up with amounts if possible, say the employer explained in English, Filipino or the employee's dialect that signing gives them up, and say the employee signed voluntarily, understood the contents, and was free of threat, duress, intimidation or undue influence. *Edi-Staffbuilders v. NLRC* (G.R. No. 145587, 26 Oct 2007): stipulations preferably in English and Tagalog or the dialect; two witnesses sign; subscribed and sworn, preferably before a DOLE, BLR or NLRC official or a labor attaché. Parts: title · venue · "KNOW ALL MEN BY THESE PRESENTS:" · employee, position, dates of employment, reason for separation · the amount and its breakdown · "in full and final settlement" · the release of named claims · the statement of explanation, understanding and voluntariness · no other claims, no admission · confidentiality only if lawful · date and place · signatures of employee and company representative · two witnesses · a **jurat** or acknowledgment per the receiving official. The case language, "subscribed and sworn", points to a jurat; some firms use an acknowledgment: which one the owner's labour counsel wants is **open**. 1 to 2 pages. Tells: a bare "I release the company from all claims", a blank or tiny amount, no itemisation, English only for a worker who reads Filipino, no witnesses, signed on the day of a termination notice. A high-risk paper for the company: always reviewed.

## Where a lawyer must look

Say these in the "Open points for counsel" page, naming the ones that apply:

| Document | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Any contract, NDA, service agreement, MOA | Liability, IP, remedies, penalties, governing law, forum |
| SPA, any power touching land, loans or surety | Art. 1878 and 1358; a wrong power voids the act |
| Resolution on borrowing, mortgage, asset sale, share issue, related-party deals | Articles, bylaws and RCC approvals; a stockholder vote may be needed |
| Secretary's certificate that a bank, the SEC or the BIR relies on | A sworn statement; a false one exposes the secretary |
| Quitclaim | The employer's burden; many grounds of invalidity |
| Employment contract with non-compete, bond, IP or fixed term | Labour standards, security of tenure |
| Demand letter that mentions criminal action | Exposure for unfounded threats; BP 22 proof rules |
| Privacy notice or consent form, data sharing terms | NPC rules; a bank client's data rules |
| Anything for a bank client | Banks often impose their own form and approvals |
| Anything to be notarised | The notary must still verify identity and willingness, and the paper must be complete |
