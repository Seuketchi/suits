# Email patterns

One pattern per kind. Each gives when to use it, the shape, and one made-up example. The rules in [EMAIL.md](EMAIL.md) apply to all of them, including *Sounds like a person* and the scan in [TELLS.md](TELLS.md). Each pattern ends with the tell that kind most often picks up.

Every shape is: **Subject**, **first line** (sentence 1, the point), **body** (only what the reader needs to act), **ask** (one, dated), **sign-off** (`Regards,` and `[Your name], [role], [company]`, the same for every kind).

Examples are made up, and every one passes the scan and shape check in [TELLS.md](TELLS.md). Word counts run from the greeting to `Regards,`.

## 1. Sending documents (cover note)

**Use when** you send a file and need one thing back.

- **Subject:** the document and its reference number, plus an action word if it helps.
- **First line:** "Attached is [document] ([type, pages]), for [purpose]."
- **Body:** the one fact the reader needs to act, if any. No summary of the contents.
- **Ask:** one, with a date.

> **Subject:** Q3 depot maintenance report for review
>
> Dear Ms. Alvarez,
>
> Attached is the Q3 maintenance report for the Northgate depot (PDF, 12 pages). Please send your comments by 14 November so we can issue the final version. The fuel figures were updated on 2 November, so please use this copy and not the draft sent on 21 October.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** "I hope this finds you well. Please find attached ...", then a paragraph retelling the report.

## 2. Request for a decision or information

**Use when** you need an answer or a choice before work can go on.

- **Subject:** the decision or the missing item.
- **First line:** the question itself, with the date you need the answer.
- **Body:** at most two options with the one fact that separates them, then your recommendation if you have one.
- **Ask:** the choice, by the date, plus what happens once they answer.

> **Subject:** Supplier choice for depot lighting
>
> Dear Mr. Okafor,
>
> Please choose a supplier by 18 November. Supplier A costs USD 4,200 and delivers in 2 weeks. Supplier B costs USD 3,600 and delivers in 6 weeks. I recommend A, because the depot opens on 9 December and B would arrive too late. We place the order the day you reply.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** "I would greatly appreciate your guidance", and each option dressed in three adjectives.

## 3. Follow-up or reminder

**Use when** a reply or action is overdue. Send it on the same thread. How long to wait is the user's call.

- **Subject:** the original subject, as a reply.
- **First line:** what you are waiting for and when you first asked.
- **Body:** the ask again, with a new date and the easiest way to answer ("a one-word reply is enough").
- **Ask:** the same single ask. Add no new ones.

> **Subject:** Re: Signed access forms for contractors
>
> Dear Ms. Reyes,
>
> On 4 November I asked for the signed access forms, and I do not have them yet. Our six contractors start on 16 November and cannot enter the site without them. Please send the forms by 13 November. A photo of each signed page is enough, and I'll confirm when they arrive.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** "Just circling back", "per my last email", and a tone of blame. Say what is missing and what it blocks.

## 4. Status update

**Use when** someone needs to know where things stand and has nothing to do.

- **Subject:** the project and "status", with the date.
- **First line:** the state in one phrase: on track, late by N days, done.
- **Body:** done, next, and any risk, each in one line. Numbers, not adjectives.
- **Ask:** "No action needed", or the one decision.

> **Subject:** Warehouse move status, 6 November
>
> Dear Mr. Santos,
>
> The warehouse move is on track to finish on 28 November, within the USD 12,000 budget. Racking is installed and stock transfer starts on 16 November. The one risk is the forklift hire, which is not yet confirmed. I will have the answer by 10 November. No action needed from you.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** "Great progress this week!", and "on time, on budget and on scope" as a three-part boast. Use the dates and the figure.

## 5. Reply to a question

**Use when** someone asked you something and you can answer.

- **Subject:** keep the thread's subject.
- **First line:** the answer. Yes, no, the number, the date.
- **Body:** the one reason or condition that matters. If you do not know, say what you will find out and by when.
- **Ask:** none, or the one follow-up question you need.

> **Subject:** Re: Can we change the delivery address?
>
> Dear Ms. Villanueva,
>
> Yes, you can change it until 12 November, when the order is packed, and there is no charge before then. Reply with the new address and I'll update the order the same day. After that date the parcel ships to the original address, and a redirect costs USD 8.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** "Great question!", "Thank you for reaching out", and repeating the question back before answering it.

## 6. Scheduling a meeting or call

**Use when** you propose a time. The slots come from the user, never from you.

- **Subject:** the purpose and the length: "30-minute call on ...".
- **First line:** why, and how long.
- **Body:** two or three slots with weekday, date, time and time zone, and the place or call link.
- **Ask:** "Please pick one, or suggest another", by a date.

> **Subject:** 30-minute call on the Q4 budget
>
> Dear Mr. Okafor,
>
> Could we have a 30-minute video call to agree the Q4 budget? I am free on Thursday 19 November at 10:00, Friday 20 November at 14:00 or Tuesday 24 November at 09:00 (all Manila time). Please tell me which suits you by 17 November, and I will send the invitation.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** "Let me know what works best for you" with no slots and no date.

## 7. Escalation of a blocker

**Use when** work is stuck and only someone with authority can free it.

- **Subject:** the blocked thing, and "blocked".
- **First line:** what is blocked, since when, and what it costs.
- **Body:** what has been tried, in one line, and who or what holds the block. State facts; no blame.
- **Ask:** the specific action they can take, by the date the cost grows.

> **Subject:** Customs release for shipment 4471 blocked
>
> Dear Ms. Alvarez,
>
> Shipment 4471 has been held at customs since 29 October, and the depot runs out of spare parts on 20 November. We sent the missing import form on 2 November and have had no answer. Please ask the customs office to release the shipment by 16 November.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** "I wanted to flag a concern" and "Unfortunately" as the opener, and blame on the people who hold the block.

## 8. Saying no or delaying

**Use when** you decline, or cannot meet a date.

- **Subject:** the thing, and the outcome: "Request cannot be met", "New date for ...".
- **First line:** the answer: no, or the date you can meet instead.
- **Body:** one true reason, only if it helps the reader. What you can offer instead, if the user gave one.
- **Ask:** none, or acceptance of the new date.

> **Subject:** New date for the audit report
>
> Dear Mr. Santos,
>
> We cannot deliver the audit report on 13 November. Two branch records arrived late, on 5 November, so the new date is 20 November. The rest of the report is finished and will not change. Please tell me by 11 November if 20 November does not work for you.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** a cushion before the no ("After careful consideration, unfortunately ..."), and "we truly value your business".

## 9. Apology or correction

**Use when** you sent something wrong. This is the one pattern that apologises, once.

- **Subject:** "Correction:" and the thing corrected.
- **First line:** what was wrong and the right fact.
- **Body:** one sentence of apology. What changed so it does not repeat, only if true.
- **Ask:** which version to use, or what to discard.

> **Subject:** Correction: invoice 2291 amount
>
> Dear Ms. Reyes,
>
> Invoice 2291, sent on 3 November, showed USD 4,200. The correct amount is USD 3,600. I am sorry for the error: the amount came from last year's price list, which we have now retired. The corrected invoice is attached (PDF, 1 page). Please discard the earlier one.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** stacked apologies ("sincerest apologies for any inconvenience this may have caused"). One apology, for the specific error.

## 10. Introduction

**Use when** you connect two people. If neither has agreed to be introduced, tell the user before they send.

- **Subject:** both names and the reason: "Introduction: Ms. Alvarez and Mr. Okafor".
- **First line:** who you are connecting and why.
- **Body:** one line of role for each person, and what each can do for the other.
- **Ask:** name who writes first, and step back.

> **Subject:** Introduction: Ms. Alvarez and Mr. Okafor
>
> Dear Ms. Alvarez and Mr. Okafor,
>
> I am introducing you because you both plan fleet maintenance. Ms. Alvarez runs the Northgate depot. Mr. Okafor leads maintenance at Harbor Freight. Ms. Alvarez, please write to Mr. Okafor by 13 November about sharing spare parts, since both depots stock the same brake pads. I'll leave you to it.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** praise for both people ("two brilliant professionals") and "I'm thrilled to connect you". Give each person's role and the reason instead.

## 11. Hand-over

**Use when** you pass work or a responsibility to someone.

- **Subject:** what is handed over, to whom, from when.
- **First line:** what you hand over and the date it becomes theirs.
- **Body:** where the material is, the open items (three at most) and who to ask now.
- **Ask:** confirm receipt, by a date. Past 125 words, put the detail in an attached note.

> **Subject:** Hand-over of depot reporting from 16 November
>
> Dear Mr. Santos,
>
> The monthly depot reporting becomes yours on 16 November. The files are in the shared folder `Depot/Reporting`. Two items are open: the October fuel figures and the late branch records. Ms. Reyes can answer questions about both, and I am away from 16 November. Please confirm you have access by 12 November.
>
> Regards,

**Watch for:** "It has been a pleasure", and a closing line that sums up the hand-over. End on the confirmation.
