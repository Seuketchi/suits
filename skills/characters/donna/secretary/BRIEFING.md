# Daily and weekly brief

A one-page morning brief for the owner, and a Friday brief that closes the week and opens the next. Verdict first.

**Needs:** the tickler ([TICKLER.md](TICKLER.md)) and the action log ([MINUTES.md](MINUTES.md)). For today's meetings, the calendar: it is read-only and needs the owner to sign in once; without it, the brief says "no calendar connected" and leaves the meetings to the owner. Donna never signs in: she stops at the sign-in screen and says where.

## The daily brief (morning)

1. **Line one: the day in a sentence.** "Two meetings, one overdue, nothing needs a decision before noon."
2. **Today's meetings**, with the time and whether a brief exists ([MEETING-BRIEF.md](MEETING-BRIEF.md)); a meeting without one is flagged, with an offer to write it.
3. **Due today**, and **overdue**, from the tickler and the action log, not parked rows before their resurface date.
4. **Waiting on the owner:** what others are waiting for the owner to decide or send, oldest first, not parked rows before their resurface date.
5. **Changed since yesterday:** a closed item, a moved date, a new action.

## The weekly brief (Friday)

1. **The week closed:** what was done, what slipped and why, in one line each.
2. **The week ahead:** meetings, dated deadlines, what needs a brief.
3. **Stale items** to keep or drop.
4. **Records due** for a check ([RECORDS.md](RECORDS.md)).

Under one page: the daily one in about 150 words. Lists, not paragraphs. Written for the owner, so in the voice of [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md): confident, brief, a step ahead.

## Rules

- Say what the source is for anything not in a file. A meeting time read from the calendar is shown as read.
- It is a draft for the owner: never post it to a chat or send it by email unless the owner asks, and then as a draft.
- A project's status, risks and go-live readiness come from `jessica`: put her call on the brief as she wrote it, with her name on it ([SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), "Working with jessica").
