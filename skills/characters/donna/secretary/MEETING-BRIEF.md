# Meeting brief

One page the owner reads before a meeting. Verdict first: what the meeting is for and what the owner should walk out with. A three-page brief goes unread.

**Needs:** the invite or an agenda text, the last minutes, and the action log ([MINUTES.md](MINUTES.md)). The calendar helps and is optional; it is not signed in unless the owner signs in. Ask for nothing else: a gap is a placeholder.

## Contents, in this order

1. **Purpose and the decision wanted**, in two lines at the top. Mark each agenda item **decide** or **discuss**.
2. **Attendees**, one line each: who they are and why they are in the room.
3. **Agenda**, each item with its time and its owner.
4. **What was decided last time** on each topic, with the date, from the last minutes.
5. **Open actions** from the log for these attendees: Owner, Task, Due, done or not. An overdue one is flagged first, in plain words ("Overdue since 4 November").
6. **Pre-reads**, one line each: what it is and the two-sentence point of it. A file not read is said so.
7. **Questions the owner may be asked**, and what the owner has already said or promised on the topic, only from files.

Build it as a one-page memo with [templates/memo.html](../docs/templates/memo.html) or plain text in chat, as the owner prefers. Page 1 must fit on one page: cut the pre-read lines before cutting an open action.

## Rules

- Everything in it comes from a file or from the owner's words; anything from memory is marked **unchecked**. Never write what the owner "will say" or "agrees to": that commits the owner. A position is the owner's own, quoted from a file, or left as a question.
- Confidential material stays in the owner's files ([SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), hard rules). Redact nothing by hiding it under a box: leave it out.
- Ready the day before when the meeting is known; otherwise on request.
- The brief is a draft: never send it to the attendees.

## Standard

One-page briefs with attendees and why each is there, the agenda with times, prior decisions, open actions (owner, due, done) and a line per pre-read, and open items pulled and overdue ones flagged before the next meeting, come from a practitioner playbook (Granola, "Setting up meeting briefs"): VERIFIED, secondary, a vendor's opinion and not a standard. The O\*NET task for executive secretaries, "prepare agendas and arrangements for meetings" (importance 73), supports the agenda. Marking decide against discuss is practice, UNCONFIRMED.
