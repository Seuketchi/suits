# Pitching

## Core Principle

Audiences retain 3–4 points. Identify them first — every word in the pitch must serve one of them.

## Before Writing Anything: The Vertebrae

Name the 3–4 things the audience must remember after the pitch ends. Write those down. Then build the pitch around them. If a section doesn't support a vertebra, cut it.

## The 2-Sentence Test

Can you describe the product in 2 sentences that a non-technical person immediately understands? If not, the pitch is not ready. Clarity beats completeness — 80% accurate, 100% understandable.

## Structure (5–10 min pitch)

| Section | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Opening one-liner | 20–30 sec | State what it is OR lead with traction — not both |
| Problem | 1–1.5 min | Real data, real quotes — never fabricated scenarios |
| Why current solutions fail | 30–45 sec | One sharp insight, not a list |
| Solution | 1 min | Simple mechanism, simply stated |
| Live demo | 1–2 min | Show it working — narrate actions, not features |
| Traction | 30–45 sec | Always include timeframes with numbers |
| Market | 30 sec | Bottoms-up sizing — no raw TAM without capture logic |
| Business model | 30 sec | Show recurring revenue logic |
| Team | 15–20 sec | Only relevant credentials — accomplishments, not titles |
| Ask | 30 sec | Explicit, specific, no hedging |
| Close | 20 sec | Restate vertebrae as conviction, not summary |

## Opening: Two Options

- **Clarity lead** — 2-sentence description of the product
- **Traction lead** — most impressive stat with timeframe

For mixed audiences: clarity first, traction immediately after.

## Traction Rule

Never give absolute numbers without timeframes. "35 responses from one post" is stronger than "35 responses."

## The Ask

State exactly what you want. A soft ask ("we're open to discussions") signals low conviction. Force a yes-or-no response.

## Mixed Audience (Judges You Can't Predict)

Lead the pitch for clarity. Adjust in Q&A based on who asks.

- Investors → traction, market size, business model
- Academics → methodology, evidence, data sourcing
- Institutional admin → campus specificity, deployability, pilot scope

## Common Mistakes

- Starting with the team slide — earn that credibility with the problem first
- Fabricating scenarios — only use real data and real quotes
- Explaining the implication of your data — if the data is strong, it's self-evident
- Soft ask or no ask
- Turning to read slides during delivery

## Slide Design

- One point per slide
- 7 words of text maximum per slide
- No videos — live demo only
- Important content at top of slide
